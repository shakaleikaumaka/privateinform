Abstract

The P.I.T. Protocol — Public Information Transmission — turns a gathering's ephemeral knowledge into a permanent public good. But its fifth and final stage, Consent-First Governance, declares that nothing enters the permanent public archive without explicit consent. Which raises the question the public paper left standing: where does the signal live before consent arrives — and where does it live if consent never does? This paper answers it. That place is the Private P.I.T. — Private Information Transmission — the consent-sealed holding layer of the same protocol. Same soul, opposite direction: the public pit broadcasts to everyone, forever; the private pit holds for someone, until.

§1 · The Twin Protocol

One protocol, two directions.

Every gathering produces two kinds of signal. The kind the room agrees to give the world — talks, transcripts, the public record — and the kind that is not yet, or not ever, the world's to hold: the unlisted call, the half-formed dream, the friend's phone number, the draft that names a real person, the secret that keeps a gift a surprise.

The Public P.I.T. exists for the first kind. The Private P.I.T. exists for the second kind — and it is not a lesser pit. It is the load-bearing half of the protocol's soul, because a protocol that promises "nothing public without consent" must also answer: then where does it rest, and who guards the door?

§2 · Why a Private P.I.T. Must Exist

Consent arrives after the signal does.

In the life of every archive there is an interval — sometimes an hour, sometimes forever — between receiving knowledge and being permitted to share it. A call is recorded before its guests have blessed the transcript. A collaborator shares a draft before the world may see it. A friend trusts you with something that was never meant to be content at all.

Without a declared private layer, that interval is where protocols leak: a draft committed to a public repo "temporarily," a transcript synced to the wrong folder, a name published before its person was asked. The Private P.I.T. makes the interval a first-class citizen. It is the protocol looking its own shadow in the eye and giving it a home, a lock, and a law.

"Nothing enters the permanent public archive without explicit consent. This is not a feature of the protocol. This is the protocol's soul." — The P.I.T. Protocol, Stage 5 · the law the Private P.I.T. enforces from the other side
§3 · The Five Laws of the Private P.I.T.

The vault discipline.

I

Consent:pending by default

Everything that enters a Private P.I.T. is marked consent:pending the moment it arrives. Nothing inside is assumed shareable — not by proximity, not by friendship, not by silence. Silence is not consent. Consent is a word, spoken by its owner, and only that word moves a signal from the private pit to the public one.

II

The Standing Consent Window

Every person whose signal rests in a Private P.I.T. holds a permanent, revocable window over it: one word and it is released; one word and it is destroyed; one word and it is sealed forever. The window never expires and is never negotiated. The archive serves its people, not the other way around.

III

Fail closed

When in doubt, the door does not open. If a key is missing, a permission unclear, a provenance uncertain — the Private P.I.T. shows nothing, serves nothing, and says why to its keeper and no one else. A vault that fails open is not a vault. Uncertainty always resolves toward silence.

IV

Leave no trace in the open

Sealed material is never placed where history cannot forget it. No secrets in public git history, no ciphertext stranded in immutable mirrors, no "temporary" public copies. Rotation must always be possible: a lock whose old keys can never be retired is a lock already broken. Holdability requires deletability.

V

Released with love, or never

The only lawful exits from a Private P.I.T. are release (the owner blesses transmission, and the signal graduates to the Public P.I.T. with honors) and return (the signal goes back to its owner, or is destroyed at their word). There is no third door. A gift is given, never taken — and a secret is kept, never spent.

§4 · The Lifecycle of a Private Signal

Down into the vault, up into the light — or home.

  1. Receive 🤲

    A signal is trusted to the pit — a call recording, a draft, a name, a dream. It is stamped consent:pending at the threshold, before it is even read twice.

  2. Seal 🔒

    It rests behind the vault discipline: minimal copies, locked permissions, no public mirrors, keeper-only access. The fewer hands, the stronger the hold.

  3. Hold 🕳️

    The pit waits — an hour, a season, forever. Holding is not idleness; it is the active work of not transmitting: resisting every "just this once," every convenience, every leak-shaped shortcut.

  4. Release 🕊️ or Return 🌊

    The owner's word arrives. If it is yes, the signal graduates to the Public P.I.T. — consent recorded, provenance intact, transmitted with pride. If it is no or never, the signal is returned or destroyed, and the pit lets it go without a copy.

  5. Transmit 📡 (only after release)

    Graduated signals join the public archive under the full ceremony of the Public P.I.T. — the Knowledge Transponder, the radical honesty audit, the CC0 gift. The two pits meet here, at the consent boundary, and only here.

§5 · One Window Governs Both Pits

The consent window never sleeps.

The P.I.T. universe keeps a single universal consent window — consent@publicinform.com — and it governs the private pits exactly as it governs the public ones. One word — remove — and you vanish from any archive, public or private, no questions, honored with love. One word — release — and what was held becomes a gift to the world. The window is checked every morning. Takedown requests never sleep in the inbox.

Every fork of either protocol inherits this law. A pit without a consent window is not a pit — it is a pile.
